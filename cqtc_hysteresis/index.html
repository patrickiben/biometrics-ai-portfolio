SAP and mock shells: a prespecified check

C-QTc hysteresis check: a signed loop area on model residuals

Tools builtRuns: cqtc_hysteresis/R/run_oc.RExploratory (synthetic)

Synthetic data only: simulated for this page; not trial data and not a regulatory deliverable.

Tools built: a small formalization of the standard C-QTc hysteresis check. The signed loop area is established (cyclicity analysis; the hysteresis-loop literature). This page computes it on residuals from the prespecified direct-effect model and documents its operating characteristics on synthetic data. It supplements, and does not replace, the prespecified C-QTc model or effect-compartment modeling.

The SAP step this formalizes

Concentration-QTc (C-QTc) analysis under ICH E14 Q&A (R3) (Q5.1) fits a prespecified model of ddQTcF (the change from baseline in QTcF minus the time-matched placebo mean) on plasma concentration, usually a linear mixed-effects model (Garnett et al. 2018). That model assumes the effect follows concentration with no delay. Before fitting it, the SAP checks for hysteresis: an effect that lags concentration, from distribution to the site of action, a metabolite or slow channel kinetics.

The check is usually visual. The registry SAPs listed under credit assess it with joint plots of the mean ddQTcF and mean concentration by time and a "hysteresis plot", and consider other models if "the curve of the hysteresis plot shows a counterclockwise loop" and the time of the peak effect is more than 1 hour from tmax. The CiPA Phase 1 SAP adds a formal one-sided Wilcoxon test at 1% alongside the same 1 hour rule. This page replaces the visual judgment with one number per participant and a test with a known size, and keeps the tmax gap beside it.

Statistic

Data. For each active participant i: actual times t1 < ... < tm over the dosing interval, concentration Cij and ddQTcFij. Concentration is scaled to the participant's own Cmax, xij = Cij / maxj Cij (prespecified).

Direct-effect model (prespecified, no time factor): ddQTcFij = f(Cij; theta) + bi + eij, with f linear (theta0 + theta1 C), or Emax (E0 + Emax C / (EC50 + C)) if the SAP's linearity check selects it. Residuals rij = ddQTcFij - f-hat(Cij) - b-hati.

Signed area of the closed path (xi1, ri1), ..., (xim, rim), back to the start (the shoelace formula, index m + 1 = 1):

Ai = ½ Σj=1..m (xj rj+1 - xj+1 rj)

Ai > 0 means the loop runs counterclockwise: the effect is lower on the way up than on the way down, so it lags concentration. Ai is in ms. It is also the off-diagonal of the antisymmetric part of the lag-1 cross-moment Γ = Σj zj zj+1T with zj = (xj, rj), so reversing time flips its sign exactly.

Test. The statistic is A-bar, the mean of Ai over participants. Under the null (no delay given f, with e independent of C and symmetric), each Ai is symmetric about 0, because Ai is linear in ei; autocorrelation within a participant is allowed. A participant-level sign-flip test is therefore exact: all 2n sign vectors when n ≤ 20, otherwise B = 9,999 random sign vectors with p = (1 + #{A-bar* ≥ A-bar}) / (B + 1). One-sided for counterclockwise loops; two-sided, on |A-bar|, also catches clockwise loops (tolerance). Secondary: a parametric bootstrap from the fitted direct-effect model (B = 999).

Reported beside the test: A-bar in ms with a t-based 95% CI, the model used for f, and the tmax gap (the time of peak ddQTcF minus the median tmax, flagged when it exceeds 1 hour). Two versions of the gap are computed: the median over participants of the time of peak ddQTcF, and the time of the peak of the mean ddQTcF profile, as in the registry SAPs.

Why the model matters

The closed-path area of any straight line (x, a + b x) is exactly 0, and a participant-level constant adds no area. So under a linear f the residual area equals the raw area of ddQTcF itself, whatever the fitted slope and intercepts. A curved direct effect is different. Sampled at 12 times, the path up and the path down pass through different points of the curve and the closing segment cuts a chord, so the raw path encloses area with no delay at all: 1.40 ms for the noise-free typical participant under the Emax model, against -0.002 ms on a 0.01 h grid and 2.34 ms for a real delay (linear, ke0 = 0.7 per h).

With noise and 32 participants, the raw-area test rejects in 0.778 (MCSE 0.013) of trials with an Emax direct effect and no delay. On residuals from the Emax fit the rate is 0.032 (MCSE 0.006) (two-sided 0.040 (MCSE 0.006)). The CiPA-style linearity check (a quadratic term tested two-sided at 5%, then the Emax model kept when its AIC is lower) picks Emax in 1.000 of these trials and 0.014 of the linear ones, and the area on residuals from the f it selects rejects in 0.033 (MCSE 0.006) (Emax truth) and 0.042 (MCSE 0.006) (linear truth). When the Emax effect is also delayed (ke0 = 0.7 per h), both areas keep their power: 1.000 raw and 1.000 on Emax residuals. The residual area is conditional on the prespecified form: it removes the curvature the model describes, not curvature it misses.

Operating characteristics

Synthetic known-answer design (seed 20261025; 1,000 replicates per scenario, so the MCSE of a rate near 0.05 is 0.007). 32 active participants sampled at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 and 24 h. One-compartment oral PK with ka = 1.2 exp(N(0, 0.32)) per h, ke = 0.15 exp(N(0, 0.32)) per h and D/V = 2; an effect compartment dCe/dt = ke0 (C - Ce), solved analytically; ddQTcF = 4 ms × Ce + N(0, 52), or 20 Ce / (Ce + 0.4) ms for the Emax variant. One-sided alpha 0.05.

  • KA1, size. Linear model, direct effect: rejection 0.043 (MCSE 0.006) (two-sided 0.056 (MCSE 0.007); parametric bootstrap 0.051 (MCSE 0.007)).
  • KA2, curvature. Emax, no delay: raw area 0.778 (MCSE 0.013), Emax-residual area 0.032 (MCSE 0.006), bootstrap from the Emax fit 0.051 (MCSE 0.007).
  • KA3, power. Linear model with delay: 0.802 at ke0 = 2 per h (equilibration half-life 0.35 h), 0.991 (MCSE 0.003) at 0.7 per h (1 h) and 0.990 at 0.35 per h (2 h). Power levels off near 0.99 from ke0 = 0.7 per h (F1); a still slower effect site does not raise it further, because the effect itself then becomes smaller and flatter.
  • KA4, one SAD cohort. With 6 active participants the exact test has 64 sign vectors, so the smallest one-sided p is 1/64 = 0.016. Size is 0.044 (MCSE 0.006), and power is only 0.411 at ke0 = 0.7 per h and 0.446 at 0.35 per h. A cohort this size cannot rule a delay in or out; pooling the active cohorts, as the C-QTc model does, gives the n = 32 rows.
  • KA5, tolerance. E = 4 C - 3 M with dM/dt = 0.35 (C - M) produces clockwise loops (noise-free area -1.86 ms). The one-sided test rejects in 0.000 (MCSE 0.000) and the two-sided test in 0.859 (MCSE 0.011).
  • The tmax gap rule fires in 0.039 (individual peaks) and 0.071 (mean profile) of trials with no delay, and in 0.752 and 0.666 at ke0 = 0.7 per h, where the area test rejects in 0.991 (MCSE 0.003). In a single SAD cohort of 6 with no delay it fires in 0.266 and 0.213. It is reported beside the test, not used as the decision.

Diurnal confound and the Day -1 check

QTc has a daily rhythm. The time-matched placebo correction in ddQTcF removes it; a design or derivation that leaves part of it in can fake a loop or hide one, depending on where the rhythm's peak falls relative to tmax. NC1 adds 3 sin(2π(t + phi)/24) ms to ddQTcF under the linear direct-effect model: the one-sided test rejects in 0.437 of trials at phi = 0 h, 1.000 at phi = 18 h and 0.000 at phi = 6 h (a clockwise artifact), against about 0.05 without it. This is why the SAP requires the time-matched placebo correction.

NC2, the Day -1 check. Pair each participant's Day -1 time-matched QTcF (no drug, centered within the participant, on the same scale as the Day 1 analysis variable) with that participant's Day 1 xj and compute the same signed area. With no drug on Day -1, any loop it shows comes from timing alone. Two-sided, it rejects in 0.997 and 0.999 of trials where the residual rhythm makes the Day 1 test fail badly, and keeps nominal size without the rhythm (0.050 (MCSE 0.007)). Where the rhythm encloses less area (phi = 0 and 12 h) it detects it less often (0.325 and 0.297), and so does the Day 1 test.

SAP text

"Before the C-QTc analysis, delay between concentration and ddQTcF will be assessed by the mean signed area of each participant's concentration-ddQTcF loop, computed on residuals from the prespecified direct-effect model with concentrations scaled to each participant's Cmax, using a participant-level sign-flip test (exact for 20 or fewer participants per dose, otherwise 9,999 random sign vectors; one-sided alpha set in the SAP). The tmax gap will be reported alongside, and a Day -1 pseudo-loop check will be reported. Evidence of delay leads to consideration of an effect-compartment model."

A significant result prompts an effect-compartment model (Sheiner et al. 1979) or a metabolite explanation. It does not replace the C-QTc analysis.

What it does not do

  • It does not replace the prespecified C-QTc model, its estimate of the effect at the geometric mean Cmax, or effect-compartment modeling. It decides only whether those need a delay term.
  • It is conditional on the form of f. It removes curvature that the prespecified model describes (linear or Emax here); curvature the model misses can still look like a loop, as the raw area does under Emax.
  • The size is exact for a linear f, because a fitted line never changes the area. With an estimated Emax curve it is approximate; here it was slightly conservative (0.032 (MCSE 0.006)).
  • It does not estimate the delay. The area grows with the effect size and depends on the sampling times around tmax; it is a test, not a ke0 estimate.
  • The synthetic concentrations have no assay error, every participant shares the nominal grid, and multiple-dose steady state was not simulated. Those were not studied.
  • A residual rhythm, a period effect or any other time pattern left in ddQTcF can produce a loop with no drug delay. The Day -1 check detects such a pattern only if Day -1 is processed the same way as Day 1.
  • Nothing here is validated or a regulatory deliverable. The R and SAS shells were written by the same author (no independent double programming), the SAS is not executed, and every dataset is synthetic.
